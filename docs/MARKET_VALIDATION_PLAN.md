# FAIRWAY IQ — Lightweight Market Validation Plan

Proposed 2026-10-16. **No interviews, recruiting, outreach, survey responses, willingness-to-pay observations or pilot metrics have been collected by this task.** This plan does not authorize contacting anyone or collecting payments.

## Questions to answer

Do US amateur golfers in the 90–110 gross 18-hole segment have a recurring problem deciding how to practice? Does a simpler evidence-to-action journey improve understanding enough to earn continued use alongside or instead of an existing app? What would they give up, and what would justify payment? The premise may be wrong; feature interest alone is not validation.

## Sample and recruiting proposal

Interview ten adult US amateur golfers, targeting six to eight who self-report typical 18-hole scores of 90–110. Use the remaining places for adjacent/mostly nine-hole golfers to test boundary assumptions; keep their responses labeled rather than blending segments. Include at least four current users of competing golf apps, ideally from more than one product, plus lightweight/non-app trackers. This is a purposeful discovery sample, not a representative US market survey. Do not infer market size, conversion rates or statistical significance from ten people.

Recruit through channels the owner explicitly approves, such as known golfers or a consenting local group. Do not send messages automatically. Avoid storing precise location, account credentials, handicap identifiers or unnecessary demographics. Assign P01–P10 participant codes; keep optional contact/consent information separately with access restricted to the project owner. Obtain permission for notes; do not record audio/video by default. Propose deleting identifiable research material within 30 days of synthesis unless participants explicitly agree otherwise; this is a proposed handling policy, not an implemented service or legal compliance claim.

## Format and concise interview guide (25–30 minutes)

Start with current behavior before showing the FAIRWAY IQ concept. Tell participants that critical feedback is useful and the research is not a sales pitch.

1. **Recent context:** “Tell me about your last round: how many holes, roughly what score, and how often have you played recently?” Self-report is sufficient; do not require an official handicap.
2. **Current tracking:** “What did you record, where, and when? Which parts did you skip? Can you describe the last time that was inconvenient?” Do not ask for passwords or private account access.
3. **Competing workflow:** “Which app or other method do you use? What makes you keep it? What have you actually stopped using, and why?” Ask specifics before suggesting shortcomings.
4. **Practice decision:** “After that round, how did you decide what to work on? What did you do at the next practice session?” Separate intention from completed behavior.
5. **Frustration:** “When did your records fail to answer a question you had? How did you work around it?” Capture spontaneous problems, not agreement with a feature list.
6. **Concept comprehension:** Show a clearly labeled concept or a consenting participant's real eligible data when available. “What does this summary tell you? What can it not tell you? What would you do next?” Any synthetic research fixture is explicitly labeled and never presented as the person's performance. Do not imply the unbuilt planner/AI exists.
7. **Trade-off:** “Would you keep your current workflow, use this alongside it, or switch? What would need to be true? What information would you refuse to enter?” Explore loss of shot-level detail versus simpler input without assuming either is better.
8. **Personalized practice:** “Would a short plan change what you do next time? Tell me when you last followed a suggested drill. What would make you ignore one?” Do not count a hypothetical yes as follow-through.
9. **Payment:** Ask what they currently pay and what concrete value justified it. Then ask whether this concept would justify any payment, what range they would consider and what would make it not worth paying for. Record free-only/unknown responses. Do not anchor with invented competitor prices or count hypothetical intent as revenue.
10. **Commitment:** Offer an optional, explicitly consented future pilot/waitlist only through an approved collection method. Ask whether they would test the next eligible round and return feedback. Do not claim an interviewee enrolled unless they actually opt in.

Use neutral follow-ups: “What happened next?”, “How often in the last month?”, “Can you give an example?” Avoid “Wouldn't AI solve that?” and leading claims of guaranteed improvement.

## Recording and synthesis

For each participant record segment/holes, current workflow/app, recent concrete frustration, practice behavior, misunderstood metric, trust/privacy concern, switching reason, payment evidence category and optional pilot commitment. Separate verbatim notes (consented), researcher interpretation and product idea. Record counts with denominators and dissenting examples, not only favorable quotations. Report missing answers and differences between target/adjacent groups. No names or contact details belong in Git.

Produce an aggregate one-page findings summary: recurring problem, counterexamples, current alternatives, strongest behavior-backed signal, highest-risk assumption and one next experiment. Actual research results require a later dated document; this plan contains none.

## Proposed decision signals (not results or market benchmarks)

| Stage | Proposed signal | Interpretation / action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem discovery | At least 6 of 10 describe a recent concrete records-to-practice problem before the concept; report the 90–110 subgroup separately | Continue exploring the problem; fewer than 4 triggers revision of target/problem rather than more features; 4–5 is inconclusive |
| Comprehension task | At least 8 of 10 correctly explain a shown summary/sample limit; at least 6 identify a reasonable next action without facilitator interpretation | Test simpler explanation if missed. This does not prove coaching accuracy or lasting behavior |
| Commitment | At least 5 of 10 explicitly opt into a future approved pilot | Stronger than feature enthusiasm, still not adoption/retention or a representative rate |
| Payment evidence | At least 3 of 10 describe a concrete condition under which they would consider paying, with reasons and contrary cases recorded | A discovery signal only. No price, revenue projection or payment willingness is validated without a later real offer |
| Follow-up pilot, after V1 gates | Of enrolled/activated users, propose at least 60% complete a second synced round and review analysis within 30 days; publish raw numerator/denominator and golf opportunities | Small-cohort directional retention signal; seasonality/missing users remain visible. Not a statistical test or current result |
| V1.2 experiment, only after approval | Observe accepted-plan/completed-session counts over two weeks and ask what prevented follow-through | Validate actual practice behavior before adding AI. No completion target is established from nonexistent baseline data |

These thresholds are founder decision heuristics and may be refined before research starts; they are not scientifically validated minimums. Do not change thresholds after seeing results without disclosing the change. Never optimize engagement by hiding pending saves or inventing progress.
